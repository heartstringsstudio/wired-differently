Chapter 23

Practical Worksheets

Tools You Can Actually Use

The worksheets in this chapter are designed to be used — repeatedly, imperfectly, and without the expectation that a single completed version will solve anything permanently. Print them if that works for you. Copy them into a notebook. Adapt them. The format matters far less than the honesty of the engagement with them. A worksheet that is partially completed and genuinely used is more valuable than a perfectly filled-out version that was completed once and filed away.

Most of these worksheets are designed to be returned to periodically — weekly, monthly, or at significant transition points — rather than completed once definitively. The ADHD brain changes across conditions, seasons, and life circumstances. The worksheets that track it need to change with it.

Where worksheets reference other chapters, those chapters contain the conceptual foundation for the questions. The worksheet is the application; the chapter is the understanding that makes the application meaningful.

Worksheet 1: ADHD Strengths Inventory

Purpose: To identify the specific strengths that are present in your profile — concretely, based on actual evidence rather than aspiration. Grounded in the framework of Chapter 6.

Instructions: For each strength area, answer both questions. The first asks whether the strength is present for you. The second asks for a specific, real example — a concrete instance where this strength produced something tangible. The example is the important part; without it, the inventory becomes a wish list rather than an accurate map.

Divergent Thinking and Creativity

Is this present for you? If yes, describe a specific recent instance where you generated an idea, solution, or creative direction that was genuinely unusual — one that others hadn't reached or wouldn't have reached from the conventional starting point.

What conditions were present when this happened? (Environment, task type, level of interest, presence of others, time of day)

Pattern Recognition

Is this present for you? Describe a specific instance where you identified a pattern — in data, in a situation, in a person's behavior, in a system — that others missed or reached significantly later.

Rapid Learning

Is this present for you? Describe a domain or skill you acquired faster than expected — where the learning curve was steeper and faster than it would have been for most people with the same starting point.

What made the rapid learning possible? (Genuine interest? Clear challenge? Urgency? Novelty?)

Crisis Performance and Improvisation

Is this present for you? Describe a specific situation — professional, creative, interpersonal, practical — where urgency or unexpected disruption produced your best rather than your worst performance.

Verbal Ability and Communication

Is this present for you? Where has your verbal fluency, precision, or ability to explain complex things clearly produced a specific, tangible result?

Big-Picture Thinking and Systems Orientation

Is this present for you? Describe a situation where your ability to see the whole picture — the structural issue beneath the surface symptom, the second-order effect others weren't tracking, the systemic pattern — produced something useful.

Emotional Depth and Empathic Insight

Is this present for you? Where has your emotional perceptiveness — your ability to track what is actually happening in a situation or relationship beneath its surface — been specifically useful or meaningful?

Your Zone of Genius

Based on all of the above, describe the work, activities, or domains where you are most fully yourself — where your strengths align most clearly with the demands, and where your performance most consistently reflects your actual capacity.

What conditions need to be present for you to operate in this zone? (Environment, task structure, activation level, social configuration)

Worksheet 8: “When I'm Stuck” Troubleshooting Sheet

Purpose: A rapid diagnostic and action tool for use specifically when stuck — when a task has been on the list for too long, when you're sitting in front of something you can't start, or when functioning has ground to a halt and you don't know why. Grounded in Chapters 11, 12, and 13.

Instructions: When stuck, work through these questions in order. Stop at the first diagnosis that fits and move to its response.

First: What Is the Physical State?

  • → First action: Do not attempt cognitively demanding work. Brief walk, protein, water. If possible, a 20-minute rest. Then reassess.

A Note on Using These Worksheets

These eight worksheets are practical tools, not performance tests. A worksheet that is used messily and incompletely, returned to inconsistently, and adapted freely to your actual needs is functioning correctly. A worksheet that is completed perfectly once and then avoided because the standard set the first time is too high to repeat is not.

The ADHD brain benefits most from tools that are low-friction enough to use on difficult days, honest enough to reveal what is actually happening, and specific enough to point toward concrete actions rather than abstract intentions. These worksheets are designed to meet that standard.

Use them in the service of building the actual life you're trying to build — not as additional items on the list of things you should be doing better.
